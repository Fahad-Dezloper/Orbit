description: "A price can be right and a lender can still lose money."

## How on-chain lending works

1. A borrower deposits something valuable: the **collateral**.
2. They borrow less than it is worth, for example 80% (the **collateral factor**).
3. If prices move and the loan becomes risky, anyone may repay part of it and take some
   collateral at a discount, the **liquidation bonus** (often 5%).
4. That liquidator sells the collateral on the market to get their money back.

Lenders learn the price from an **oracle** such as Reflector, RedStone, Chainlink or Pyth.

## The hidden assumption

Step 4 only works if the market can absorb the sale. If selling the collateral pushes the price
down by more than the bonus, no liquidator will do it. The bad loan stays on the books, and the
lender takes the loss.

<Warning>
  A price is about **one** token. A lender's risk is about **all** the collateral at once. Those
  are different questions, and today only the first is answered on Stellar.
</Warning>

## What happened at YieldBlox

| Step | What happened |
| --- | --- |
| Thin market | USTRY's USDC order book traded under $1 an hour |
| One order | A single buy offer pushed USTRY from about $1.05 to $106.7 |
| Price reported | The pool's oracle read that price and passed it on, as designed |
| Money out | About $10.97M was borrowed against about $160K of USTRY |

Sources: BlockSec, Halborn, QuillAudits and Blockaid write-ups of the 22 Feb 2026 incident.

## What Stellar fixed, and what it didn't

After the hack, Reflector added AMM pools as price sources, Oracle Shield (Sun Zu Lab) added
price checks and circuit breakers, and Blend's v3 draft adds borrow limits. All three make the
**price** harder to fake. None measures how much can be **sold**.

The next loss does not need a fake price. A correctly priced token on a thin market is still
dangerous when a lender holds more of it than buyers would take.

## Why it matters most for RWAs

- Many tokenized bonds (Etherfuse's USTRY, CETES, TESOURO) trade on no outside exchange. Stellar's
  own markets are the only exit.
- Of about $3.56B of RWAs on Stellar, roughly 0.1% is used as collateral (RedStone, Oct 2026).
  Lenders cannot safely accept more without knowing how much can be sold.
