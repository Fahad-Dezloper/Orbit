---
title: "Worst of the day"
description: "Why Orbit publishes the lowest reading, not the latest."
---

Liquidity can be placed for a moment and pulled. If Orbit published its latest reading, someone
could add buyers just before a reading and remove them just after.

So the figure Orbit publishes is the **minimum over the last 24 hours**, with three rules:

| Rule | Value |
| --- | --- |
| Window | 24 hours |
| Readings needed | at least 12 |
| Largest allowed gap between readings | 2 hours |

If any rule fails, there is **no published figure** for that token and band. Orbit never fills a
gap with an old number.

## Random reading times

Readings run about every 30 minutes, at times randomized by ±25%. Nobody can know exactly when
the next reading will happen, so timing fake liquidity to it does not work.

## What this does not stop

Someone with a lot of capital could keep real buy orders in place for a whole day. Today that
is slow but cheap (the cost is mostly the interest on the parked money). The planned fixes are
in [Threat model](/security/threat-model).
