---
title: "Worst of the week"
description: "Why Orbit publishes the lowest reading, not the latest, and why it rises slowly."
---

Liquidity can be placed for a moment and pulled. If Orbit published its latest reading, someone
could add buyers just before a reading and remove them just after.

So the figure Orbit publishes is the **lowest reading of the last 7 days**, with coverage rules
on the last 24 hours:

| Rule | Value |
| --- | --- |
| Figure | lowest reading in the last 7 days |
| Readings needed in the last 24 hours | at least 12 |
| Largest allowed gap between readings | 2 hours |

If a coverage rule fails, there is **no published figure** for that token and band. Orbit never
fills a gap with an old number.

## Slow up, fast down

- **Down at once.** One low reading lowers the figure immediately.
- **Up only after a week.** A higher figure counts only once every reading for 7 days has been
  at least that high.

Someone who wants a lender to accept more of a token has to keep real buy orders in place, in
public, for a full week, where anyone can sell into them. Pulling them for a single reading
resets the clock.

## Random reading times

Readings run about every 30 minutes, at times randomized by ±25%. Nobody can know exactly when
the next reading will happen, so timing fake liquidity to it does not work.

## What this does not stop

Someone with a lot of capital could still keep real buy orders in place for a week. That now
costs a week of parked money, in the open, but it is not impossible. The remaining defences are
in [Threat model](/security/threat-model).
