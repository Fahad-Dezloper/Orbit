A price tells a lender what one token is worth. It does not tell the lender whether anyone will
buy it. Orbit measures how many dollars of each token could really be sold on Stellar right now,
and turns that into a safe limit a lending app can enforce.

This is Orbit's latest reading, straight from its public data. It refreshes every minute.

## Why it exists

When a loan goes bad, the lender has to sell the borrower's collateral to get its money back.
If the market for that token is thin, the sale fails and the lender takes the loss. On
22 February 2026 this cost the YieldBlox pool on Blend about $10.97M: one order on a market that
traded under $1 an hour moved USTRY from $1.05 to $106.7, and the pool lent against that price.

After the hack, Stellar made prices harder to fake. Nothing measured how much could actually be
sold. Orbit fills that gap.

## What Orbit gives you

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="A dollar figure, not a score">
    For each token: how many US dollars a seller could raise before the average sale price falls
    2%, 10% or 25%, and a full curve from 0.5% to 50%.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Every market on Stellar">
    The SDEX order book plus AMM pools on Aquarius, Soroswap and Sushi. For some tokens, such as
    USDY or deJAAA, nearly all of the market is in pools that order-book tools never see.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Numbers that are hard to fake">
    Real tokens only, every pool checked against what it really holds, one fair price from many
    markets, random reading times and the worst figure of the day.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="On-chain">
    The figures are published to a Soroban contract, so a lending contract can check them before
    every loan. Testnet today.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>
