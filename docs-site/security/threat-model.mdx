There are two very different ways to attack a depth figure.

| | Make Orbit lie | Change the market |
| --- | --- | --- |
| What the attacker does | Gets Orbit to report buyers that are not there | Adds real buyers, lets Orbit measure them, removes them when it matters |
| Kind of defence | Layered checks: several must fail at once | Economic: make it cost real money |
| Today | Hard | Slow, not yet expensive |

The oracle contract also bounds what can be published (no values over $1T, no timestamps more
than 60 seconds in the future or 6 hours old, no replays), and changing its admin needs two
signatures.

## Changing the market

**Timed spoofing** (adding buyers just before a reading): fixed by random reading times.

**Patient capital** (keeping real buyers for a day, then pulling them in the same ledger as a
borrow): open. $1M parked for 48 hours costs about $250 to $300 in interest. Planned fixes:

- **Slow up, fast down**: a figure may rise only after 7 days of steady depth and falls on the
  first low reading.
- **Owner limits**: count only a share of depth from any single account or LP position.
- **Age filter**: count only order-book offers resting for hours.

**Liquidity leaving under stress** (as on 10 October 2025): any trailing figure describes the
past. Orbit is best for sizing new exposure, not predicting the next hour.

## Past incidents

| Incident | Would a depth cap have helped? |
| --- | --- |
| YieldBlox, Feb 2026 | Yes: depth about $0, so cap about $0 |
| Mango Markets, Oct 2022 | Yes |
| Curve founder's CRV loans, Jul 2023 | Yes: caps block concentration |
| Aave CRV short, Nov 2022 | No: the shortfall was on the debt side |
| 10 Oct 2025 crash | No: depth vanished faster than any window |
