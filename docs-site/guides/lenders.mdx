---
title: "For lenders"
description: "Cap collateral by what the market can absorb."
---

If your protocol accepts a token as collateral, Orbit tells you how much of it you could sell if
loans go bad, and what a safe total exposure is.

## The rule

<Info>
  Never hold more of a token as collateral, across all loans, than Orbit's recommended cap. If
  Orbit has no fresh figure, accept no new exposure to that token.
</Info>

## Integrating

<Steps>
  <Step title="Pick your liquidation bonus">
    The discount you give liquidators, for example 5% (`bonus_bps = 500`).
  </Step>
  <Step title="Read the cap">
    Off-chain: [`/v1/assets/{code}/cap?bonus_bps=500`](/api/cap) returns
    `recommended_cap_usd`. On-chain: read [`fresh_depth`](/contract/reading) for the nearest
    published band and apply your own margin.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Enforce it">
    Before a deposit or borrow, check that total collateral of that token, in USD, stays at or
    below the cap. Refuse when there is no fresh figure.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Show it">
    Display the cap and how much is used to your users and risk team, using
    [`/v1/lenders`](/api/lenders) as a reference.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Blend pools

Blend v2 pools cannot change their oracle after creation, and reserve settings change through a
7-day timelock. Two practical paths:

- **New pools** can use a depth-aware oracle wrapper that returns no price (or a capped one)
  when the collateral posted exceeds Orbit's figure.
- **Existing pools** can set supply caps from Orbit's figure through governance.

Blend v3 is being designed now; depth-aware caps are a natural fit for it.

## What Orbit sees today

Orbit reads the RWA holdings of pinned Blend v2 pools (on-chain) and of Templar's markets
(reported by Templar's API), and compares them with what could be sold. See
[`/v1/lenders`](/api/lenders) and the "What lenders hold" section of the dashboard.

In November 2026 Templar's two deJAAA markets held about $4.2M of deJAAA as collateral, with a
2.5% liquidation spread. Everything on Stellar could absorb about $1.6M of deJAAA before the
price fell 10%.
