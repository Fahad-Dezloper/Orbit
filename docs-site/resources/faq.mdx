title: "FAQ"
description: "Common questions and straight answers."
---

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="The oracle gives the price. Isn't that enough?">
    No. A price is for one token; risk is about selling all the collateral. At YieldBlox the
    oracle reported a real market price and the pool still lost $10.97M, because almost nothing
    could be sold at it.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="How is this different from a price check like Oracle Shield?">
    A price check asks whether a price is trustworthy. Orbit asks how much can be sold at it. A
    real price on a tiny market is still dangerous. They work together.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="The issuer will buy tokens back. Why does depth matter?">
    Redemption takes time, needs approval and is not open to everyone. For Etherfuse tokens the
    issuer fills redemptions from the on-chain market itself. In a liquidation, the on-chain
    market is what counts.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Can someone fake liquidity?">
    Quick fakes do not work: readings are random and Orbit keeps the day's worst figure. Patient
    capital is possible today and is stated openly, with planned fixes.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Why are many tokens shown with no buyers?">
    Some are open to anyone but nobody is buying them on Stellar. Others (Franklin Templeton,
    WisdomTree, most Centrifuge funds) can only be held by approved accounts, so there is no open
    market to sell into.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Does Orbit hold or move funds?">
    No. Measurement is read-only. The only write is the publisher signing figures into Orbit's own
    oracle contract.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Is it free?">
    Yes. The data, the code (Apache-2.0) and the oracle reads are free.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>
